  I'm applying for the Senior Software Engineer role on distributed systems. The post-purchase problem is a fun one, and the scale you're working at (125 million consumers, billions of interactions) is exactly the kind of environment where reliability and careful system design actually matter. Building backends that have to stay up under real load is the work I enjoy most.

  Most of my recent work has been distributed systems and reliability. I architected a customer-facing product that runs across multiple AWS accounts on a fleet of Lambda functions; it has held 99.9\% uptime since launch and processed 2.8 million insured trips so far. I also led a small team rewriting a brittle legacy system into containerized microservices on Kubernetes with a Linkerd service mesh, with Datadog metrics wired in so we could actually see how it was behaving.

  I own four REST APIs end to end: the contracts, the versioning, and the design standards other teams build against, so owning a service's reliability and scalability is familiar territory. I work mostly in Python these days. I care about the boring parts that keep a system maintainable, like tests you can trust, clear error handling, and metrics that tell you the truth.

  I've worked at a startup before, at Arterys, a medical-imaging AI company, so the pace and the ambiguity are comfortable for me. I also like helping a team get better: I write a lot of RFCs and design guides, do plenty of code review, and mentor newer engineers.

  I'd be happy to talk more.
